Position Sizing: The 1% Risk Rule

Most traders obsess over entries and exits, but the thing that actually keeps you in the game is position sizing — how much you put on each trade. The 1% rule is the simplest, most robust way to get it right.

What the 1% rule is

The rule: never risk more than 1% of your account on a single trade. "Risk" means the amount you lose if your stop-loss is hit — not the size of the position. With a 1% risk budget you could lose 20 trades in a row and still keep about 82% of your capital. That survivability is the whole point: you cannot win long term if a bad streak wipes you out.

The formula

Position size depends on three things — your account size, your risk percentage, and the distance from entry to stop-loss:

Position size = (account × risk %) ÷ (distance from entry to stop)

This makes every loss the same fixed dollar amount, whether your stop is tight or wide. A wider stop simply means a smaller position; a tighter stop allows a larger one. Risk stays constant.

A worked example

Account: $1,000. Risk per trade: 1% = $10. You enter at $30,000 with a stop at $29,000, so your risk per coin is $1,000. Position size = $10 ÷ $1,000 = 0.01 coins, a position worth $300. If the stop is hit you lose exactly $10 — your planned 1% — no more.

How it connects to leverage and liquidation

Position sizing and leverage are two sides of the same coin. Size by risk first; if the resulting position needs heavy leverage to open, that is a signal your stop is too close or your account too small for that trade. And always make sure your stop sits well before your liquidation price — otherwise the exchange closes the trade for you, on its terms, not yours. Common mistakes

  • Risking a percentage of the position instead of the account. The rule is 1% of your whole account.
  • Moving the stop to fit a bigger position. Set the stop where your idea is wrong, then size to it — never the other way around.
  • Ignoring fees and slippage. On volatile coins, real losses can exceed the theoretical stop. Leave a small buffer.

The bottom line

Good position sizing is boring, and that is its strength. Fix your risk per trade, size every position to it, and you turn a string of losses into a survivable dip instead of a blow-up. This is education, not financial advice — trade carefully and within your means.
