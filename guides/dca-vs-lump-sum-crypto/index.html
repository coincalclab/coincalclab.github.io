DCA vs Lump Sum: Which Wins in Crypto?

Should you invest a lump sum all at once, or spread it out with dollar-cost averaging (DCA)? It is one of the most common questions in crypto. The honest answer is "it depends" — but the factors are knowable, and you can test them on real data.

The two strategies

Lump sum means putting your whole intended amount in at once. DCA means splitting it into equal buys at regular intervals — say $50 every week — regardless of price. DCA buys more coins when prices are low and fewer when they are high, smoothing your average entry.

What the maths says

Because markets rise more often than they fall over long horizons, lump sum wins on average: your money is exposed to growth for longer. Studies of traditional markets put lump sum ahead roughly two-thirds of the time. Crypto's strong long-term uptrends have historically favoured lump sum too — you can confirm this yourself with our DCA backtester, which compares regular buying against a single lump-sum entry on real price history.

Why DCA still makes sense

Averages hide risk. Lump sum wins on average, but it also has the worst-case outcome: buying the exact top right before a 70% drawdown — something crypto does regularly. DCA trades a slightly lower expected return for a much narrower range of outcomes. Its real advantages are:

  • Lower timing risk. You are never "all in" at a single price.
  • Psychological durability. A smaller, automated buy is far easier to keep doing through a crash than deploying a lump sum and watching it halve.
  • It matches how income arrives. Most people get paid monthly, not in lump sums.

How to decide

  1. Have a lump available and a long horizon and a strong stomach? Lump sum has the edge.
  2. Nervous about timing, or investing from monthly income? DCA is the practical, durable choice.
  3. Want the best of both? Many investors deploy part now and DCA the rest over a few months.

Whatever you choose, model it first. Use the backtester to see how a plan would have played out, and the DCA calculator to track your real average entry as you go.

The bottom line

DCA is not magic and lump sum is not reckless — they are different trade-offs between expected return and risk. Pick the one you can actually stick to, because the strategy you abandon in a crash is worse than either. None of this is financial advice; crypto is volatile and you can lose money.
