Risk/Reward and Win Rate

New traders obsess over win rate — the percentage of trades they win. But you can win 70% of your trades and still go broke, or win 30% and get rich. What actually matters is how win rate and risk/reward ratio work together.

The two numbers that decide everything

Long-term profitability comes down to two figures:

  • Win rate — how often your trades win.
  • Risk/reward ratio — how much you make on a winner versus how much you lose on a loser.

Neither means anything on its own. A 90% win rate is a disaster if your occasional loss is ten times the size of your wins. A 30% win rate is excellent if your winners are five times your losers.

The break-even formula

For any risk/reward ratio, there is a minimum win rate you need just to break even:

Break-even win rate = risk ÷ (risk + reward)

  • At 1:1 you need to win 50% of the time.
  • At 1:2 you need 33%.
  • At 1:3 you need only 25%.

So a better ratio buys you a lower required win rate — more room to be wrong. Our risk/reward ratio calculator works this out from your entry, stop and target instantly.

A worked example

You take 100 trades, each risking $100. Your win rate is 40% and your ratio is 1:2 (winners make $200, losers lose $100). Result: 40 wins × $200 = $8,000; 60 losses × $100 = $6,000. Net: +$2,000 — profitable despite losing most of your trades. Now flip it: a 60% win rate at 1:0.5 (winners $50, losers $100) gives 60 × $50 − 40 × $100 = −$1,000. Winning more often, losing money.

Where fees and position size fit in

Two practical adjustments turn the theory into reality. First, fees and slippage raise your real break-even win rate slightly — always leave a margin. Second, none of this survives a single oversized loss, which is why you pair it with disciplined position sizing so every loss is the same controlled fraction of your account. The maths only compounds in your favour if you are still in the game.

The bottom line

Stop chasing a high win rate for its own sake. Aim for a risk/reward ratio whose break-even win rate is comfortably below the win rate you can realistically achieve — then size every trade so no single loss can derail you. This is education, not financial advice.
