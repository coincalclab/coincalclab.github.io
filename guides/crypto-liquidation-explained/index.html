Crypto Liquidation Explained

Liquidation is the moment an exchange force-closes a leveraged position because the losses have eaten through the margin backing it. It is the single most common way leveraged traders lose money. This guide explains exactly how it works and how to keep it from happening to you.

What liquidation actually is

When you open a leveraged position, you only put up a fraction of its value — the margin. The exchange effectively lends you the rest. If the price moves against you, your losses are deducted from that margin. Once the remaining margin falls to the maintenance margin — the minimum the exchange requires to keep the position open — the exchange closes the position automatically to protect itself. That forced close is a liquidation, and it usually means losing most or all of the margin you committed.

How the liquidation price is calculated

For an isolated-margin position, a close approximation is:

  • Long: liquidation price ≈ entry × (1 − 1/leverage + maintenance margin rate)
  • Short: liquidation price ≈ entry × (1 + 1/leverage − maintenance margin rate)

The key insight is the 1/leverage term. At 2× leverage you can survive roughly a 50% move against you; at 10× only about 10%; at 50× barely 2%. Higher leverage drags the liquidation price right up next to your entry, so even normal volatility can wipe you out.

A worked example

Say you go long Bitcoin at $30,000 with 10× leverage and a 0.5% maintenance margin. Plugging in: 30,000 × (1 − 0.1 + 0.005) = $27,150. A drop of just 9.5% liquidates the position — and Bitcoin moves more than that in a single day on a regular basis.

Why exchanges' numbers differ slightly

Real exchanges use tiered maintenance margins (the rate rises for bigger positions), charge funding and fees, and offer cross-margin (which pools your whole balance as backing). These shift the exact trigger a little, but the formula above gets you within a fraction of a percent for a typical isolated position.

Five ways to avoid liquidation

  1. Use less leverage. This is the biggest lever. Dropping from 20× to 5× quadruples the move you can survive.
  2. Set a stop-loss well above the liquidation price. A stop closes the trade on your terms, at a price you chose, instead of the exchange's forced price.
  3. Size positions by risk, not by gut. Decide the maximum you are willing to lose first, then work backwards. Our position size calculator does this for you.
  4. Keep spare margin. Adding margin pushes the liquidation price further away — but never add margin just to avoid admitting a losing trade.
  5. Account for funding and fees. On long holds these costs quietly erode your margin and move liquidation closer.

The bottom line

Liquidation is not bad luck — it is the predictable result of too much leverage relative to an asset's volatility. Know your liquidation price before you enter, keep leverage modest, and always trade with a stop. Crypto trading carries substantial risk of loss; never trade with money you cannot afford to lose.
